## Design decisions and flags for hand-off

1. **"Power grip" is never a cloze answer.** The name is semantically self-descriptive: "power" = "maximum force," so describe-to-name collapses (a non-knower coins the name from the visible purpose) and name-to-meaning is equally decodable in reverse. Either direction is a giveaway, so the two-way-definition default is deliberately NOT applied. The name rides visible and bold on C1 as the anchor; if Parker wants name-production anyway, the least-leaky cue is the end-state description (P7), not the purpose.
2. **The end-state (P5–P7) is Back Extra, not a card.** Considered as a §12d named-end-position card ("underside on the {{curved palm}}, fingers over the {{top}}"), but both blanks are largely derivable from visible palm-up geometry (palm up, so the handle rests on the palm and the fingers close over the top) — padding-adjacent under rule 20's spirit. Kept as C1's confirmation-cue teaching instead. Editor may overrule.
3. **Thumb position differs by phase** — extended upward during insertion (P4), curled over the top in the final grip (P5). The two Back Extra lines keep the phases separate; do not merge them into "thumb extended" as a grip description.
4. No unmarked-context temptations beyond the slip-consequence chain (P8), which is used as licensed Back Extra support.
