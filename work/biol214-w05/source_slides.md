## Slide 3
- Bone Structure
- Bone markings are divided into two landmark categories: ‘depressions’ or ‘projections’
- Projections usually serve as attachment points for ligaments and tendons
- Internal Structure
- Spongy: trabeculae
- Better manage stress from multiple directions
- Compact (load bearing): densely packed hydroxyapatite
- Bone Cavities
- Red marrow: site of RBC production
- Yellow marrow: filled with adipose
  SPEAKER NOTES:
  > Study Tips
  > Long bones have a proximal &amp; distal epiphysis and only one diaphysis
  > Be sure you can distinguish between the proximal and distal epiphysis
  > Be familiar with labeling this figure

## Slide 4
- Bone (Osseous) Tissues
- Osteon: basic structural unit
- Central canal: contains nerves and vasculature
- Concentric lamellae
- Pockets in concentric lamellae are lacuna and contain osteocytes
- Lacuna are connected to central canal via canaliculi
  SPEAKER NOTES:
  > Study Tips
  > Be familiar with these figures!
  > Let's breakdown the osteon starting from its central canal and move outwardly
  > Central canal: conduit for vasculature and nerves
  > Concentric lamellae: they are called concentric because they are made up of layered circles that surround a shared center aka surround the central canal
  > Lacunae: pockets within concentric lamellae
  > Osteocytes: cells within lacunae that make bone
  > Canaliculi: connect lacunae to central canal to receive nutrients

## Slide 6
- Bone Growth
- Growth occurs  between the diaphysis and the epiphysis in the epiphyseal plate
- Epiphyseal plate contains cartilage until growth is complete
- Cartilage growth occurs on distal end of plate while bone is added medially
- Diaphysis lengthens
- When growth is complete, the plate becomes the epiphyseal line
  SPEAKER NOTES:
  > Study Tips
  > Be sure you explain the difference between the epiphyseal plate vs. epiphyseal line
  > Epiphyseal Plate: made of cartilage and marks boundary of bone growth
  > Epiphyseal Line: made of bone and marks end of bone growth (see slide 3 for image of epiphyseal line)
  > Epiphyseal plate is often referred to as the “growth plate”

## Slide 20
- Axial Skeleton: Thoracic Cage
- Thoracic cage protects vital thoracic organs
- Sternum is three fused bones
- Manubrium, Body of sternum, and Xiphoid Process
- Ribs attached to sternum via costal cartilages
- True: ribs 1-7
- False: 8-12
- Floating ribs 11-12
  SPEAKER NOTES:
  > Study Tips
  > Sternum made of three fused bones (be able to identify each)
  > Manubrium
  > Body of Sternum
  > Xiphoid Process
  > Be comfortable identifying landmarks of sternum (jugular notch, sternal angle, etc.)
  > Understand number of ribs and their denominations
  > True ribs (1-7) : attach directly to sternum via their own costal cartilage
  > False ribs (8-12) : do NOT attach directly to sternum via their own costal cartilage (ribs 8,9, &amp; 10 share a costal cartilage)
  > Floating Ribs (11 &amp; 12) : do NOT attach to sternum
  > They need to know the sternum and rib denominations, they do not need to know the markings on each rib.
  > (sternum=3 fused bones, their names)
  > (ribs=#of true, false and floating)
  > True=First 7 pairs, connect directly to the sternum
  > False=Next 3 pairs (8,9,10), connect to lowest true rib, NOT directly to sternum
  > Floating=Last 2 pairs (11,12) only connected to spine, appear to ‘float’
