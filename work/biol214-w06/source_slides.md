## Slide 5
- Upper Limb: Arm: Humerus
- Orientation: Head is directed medially while deltoid tuberosity is angled laterally.  Olecranon fossa is located posteriorly.
  MEDIA: image5.png, image6.png
  SPEAKER NOTES:
  > Study Tips
  > Be able to identify the bolded labels
  > How to differentiate between right and left?
  > Deltoid tuberosity is lateral
  > Humoral head is medial
  > Greater and lesser tubercle seen from anterior view
  > Greater tubercle is more lateral
  > Other helpful connections
  > Trochlea of Humerus articulates with Trochlear Notch of Ulna
  > Olecranon Fossa of Humerus articulates with Olecranon Process of Ulna
  > Coronoid Fossa of Humerus articulates with Coronoid Process of Ulna
  > Capitulum of Humerus articulates with Head of Radius

## Slide 6
- Upper Limb: Arm: Radius and Ulna
- Orientation: The ulna is medial while the radius is lateral. The radial tuberosity is directed medially and rough surface on distal end is posterior.  The ulnar styloid process is medial while the radial styloid is lateral.  Trochlear notch faces anteriorly.
  MEDIA: image7.png, image8.png
  SPEAKER NOTES:
  > Study Tips
  > Be able to identify the boxed labels
  > Ulna has a “claw” at proximal end
  > Radius has a circular structure at proximal end
  > Differentiate between right and left
  > Styloid process of radius is lateral
  > Smooth surface of distal radius is anterior
  > Trochlear notch of Ulna faces anteriorly

## Slide 8
- Pelvic Girdle
- Orientation: Acetabulum is directed laterally, ischial spine points posteriorly, and connection of pubic bones at pubic symphysis is located anteriorly.
- The os coxae is formed by the fusion of
- three bones: the ilium, ischium, and pubis.
- *Do not worry about the labels on this slide
  MEDIA: image10.gif
  SPEAKER NOTES:
  > Study Tips
  > Be able to identify the labels
  > Pubic Symphysis is the articulation between left and right pubic bones
  > How to differentiate left vs right?
  > Acetabulum is always facing laterally--> articulates with the head of the femur
  > Pubic tubercle faces anteriorly
  > The "hip bone" that you can feel is the anterior superior iliac spine

## Slide 11
- Orientation: Head is directed medially while greater trochanter is directed laterally.  The intercondylar fossa is located posteriorly while patellar surface is anterior.
- Lower Limb: Leg: Femur
  MEDIA: image13.png
  SPEAKER NOTES:
  > Study Tips
  > Be able to identify the bolded labels
  > Head of the femur articulates with the acetabulum of the ox coxae
  > How to differentiate between right and left?
  > Head is directed medially
  > Linea aspera is on posterior surface of femur
  > Intercondylar fossa is on distal portion of posterior femur
  > Patellar surface is on distal anterior surface
  > You do NOT need to know the intertrochanteric line, intertrochanteric crest, the medial and lateral supracondylar lines, or adductor tubercle

## Slide 13
- Lower Limb: Leg: Tibia and Fibula
- Orientation: Fibula is lateral while the tibia is medial
- Fibular malleolus is lateral while Tibial malleolus is medial.  Tibial tuberosity is anterior.
  MEDIA: image15.png
  SPEAKER NOTES:
  > Study Tips
  > Be able to identify the bolded labels (except for fibular notch)
  > Orientation
  > Fibula is Lateral (the ”la” in fibula can remind you that it is lateral)
  > Tibia is medial
  > How to distinguish right vs left?
  > Tibia has medial malleolus on distal end
  > Fibula has lateral malleolus on distal end
  > Tibial tuberosity is anterior
  > “Sharp” surface of Fibula faces anteriorly

- Lateral Cuneiform
- Cuboid
- Mnemonic: The Circus Needs More Interesting Little Clowns.
  MEDIA: image16.gif
  SPEAKER NOTES:
  > Study Tips
  > Be able to identify the bolded labels
  > How to use mneumonic?
  > Start on proximal portion of foot at talus
  > Move down to calcaneus
  > Move medially and distally towards big toe
  > Move laterally towards little toe
  > Bones: Tarsal bones, metatarsal, phalanges
  > Tarsal bones:
  > Talus, calcaneus, navicular, medial cuneiform, intermediate cuneiform, lateral cuneiform, cuboid
  > Metatarsal bones:
  > I-V (beginning with hallux) – tuberosity of 5th metatarsal
  > Phalanges:
  > I-V, proximal, middle & distal (except hallux has no middle phalanx)
