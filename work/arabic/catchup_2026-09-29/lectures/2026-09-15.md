## Her key teaching points (for card notes)

- **Waaw/yaa are consonants in exactly 3 cases** (1001-1648, board): at the START of a word (waajib, yajib); the FIRST of two vowel letters together (jawaab, buyuut, thiyaab); carrying a SUKUUN (bayt, khawkh, khayr). 'The good thing is they're consistent' (3274-3281).
- **Unspoken tashkiil rules** (1452-1500): no mark on the first letter = assume fatHa; no mark on the last letter = assume sukuun. Some words start with Damma (buyuut) or kasra (Hijaab).
- **Kasra after an alif sounds like 'e'**: baa-Heth, waa-jeb (846-880, 1815-1850). (The nightly record says the alif changes - it is the kasra.)
- **Book error**: the book says waaw/yaa are consonants when 'preceded by or followed by' a vowel; she says FOLLOWED by only - 'probably there is a mistake there' (3302-3380).
- **khawkh = plum**, not the book's 'peach' (1592-1607); khawkh and bayt are REQUIRED because they are in the writing exercise (1608-1620).
- **cayn** (not yet taught, in shaaric): 'try to say ah and then try to shock yourself'; exaggerate it so it's not a hamza (3638-3694).
- **akhbaar vs akbar**: 'it's akh, not ak'; akbar = greatest (3805-3825).
- **Habiibi/Habiibti etiquette**: same gender = fine; opposite gender = fiancee/girlfriend only; fine with kids ('I use it with my dogs') (3900-3955).
- **SabaaH al-khayr -> SabaaH an-nuur** ('I wish you an enlightened morning'); unlike marHaba you don't just say it back (4044-4115).
- **al-Hamdu lillaah** alone already means 'I'm good'; her OPTIONAL Christian alternative **nashkur ar-rabb** 'to minimize the name of Allah in our speech' (4250-4415).
- **Gendered adjectives like Spanish**: jayyid/jayyida, haadhaa/haadhihi (4419-4462).
