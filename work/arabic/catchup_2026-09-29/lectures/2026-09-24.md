## Letters (notes for the letter cards)

- **ء** - Hamza, second pass (framed as staged: 'Hamza is so complicated in Arabic, so we're going to give it to you gradually ... we'll leave it until the end of the semester'). Never written bare at the start of a word: 'at the beginning of the word it has to have a seat ... the only seat is alif.' Vowel on the alif-hamza decides the sound: fatHa = a, Damma = u, kasra = i and 'it has to be written under the alif.' Alif-hamza can carry a sukuun, 'but we don't write the sukuun.' 'It's a glottal stop.' 'Hamza is not considered a short vowel, it's considered a consonant - that's why it takes fatHa, Damma, kasra and sukuun.' In the middle/end it can sit on alif (ta'ta'a) or 'on the line' after a long alif (baa'): 'we say this is hamza on the line.' Scope: 'For now, what I need you to do: recognize it and learn how to read it ... Later ... we will learn the whys and the hows.'
- **ا** - At the start of a word alif is only a seat for hamza ('the only seat is alif'); a student naming the picture 'alif hamza' was 'more accurate' than 'alif'. In letter names like baa' the hamza comes 'after the alif, on the line.' She pronounces the name close to 'alef'.
- **ب** - Letter name is baa' WITH a final hamza on the line: 'sometimes out of laziness you say baa, but the correct name of the letter is baa' with a hamza.' Vowel demo on baa: 'ba, bu, bi - they have vowels; with the sukuun, b - so it's silent.' Dictation item 5 was the name باء. Warm-up: 'give me a word with a baa'' -> baab, baabaa, baytii.
- **ت** - Contrast with the ending t of feminine words: 'Now look at the Arabic, it's a taa' but different than the taa' that you learned. This one occurs only at the end of the word, so we'll learn it soon' (taa' marbuuTa, ة). In ukhtuhu: 'we added the Damma on the taa' and then we added hu.' In Drill 2 atat: 'I want a quick t here.' Also contrasted with the 'harsh T' (ط) of Taalib: 'capital T ... not just t.'
- **ث** - Dictation item 1 was the letter name ثاء (thaa'), written by Weston. Letter names end in hamza on the line.
- **خ** - akh = 'alif hamza with a fatHa and khaa'' (brief; the letter name is not clearly on tape). akh / ukht / ukhtuhu drilled.
- **ي** - In ustaadhatii: 'some feminine ends with a taa' and a yaa' ... the ii here refer to me' - final yaa' as the long ii of 'my'.
- **ذ** - Shaami habit: 'Even the dh, we don't like it, we turn it into z' - ustaadh -> istaaz. She sometimes says 'ustaaz' even for the formal word; the formal sound is dh (as in 'the').
- **ز** - Same note as ذ: in Shaami dh often becomes z (istaaz).
- **ر** - As of 9/24 she treats raa' as not yet known: 'I'll read the first one [ibHaar] because you don't know the last letter.'
- **ح** - Not named this class. Clean examples of the sound inside words: baHr (sea) and ibHaar (sailing).
- **ج** - Not named this class. Her j in jaamica (Levantine speakers often soften it toward zh).
- **د** - Not named this class. Heard inside duktuur (doctor).
- **و** - Not named this class. Heard as the word wa (and) and as long uu in tafaDDaluu / akhuuhu (the final alif of tafaDDaluu is silent).
