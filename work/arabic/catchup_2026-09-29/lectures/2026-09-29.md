## What happened

- **Phone only, before Teams started (phone 0-739 s):** students practiced numbers; her 'Marhaba!', prayer; Global Focus Week guest speakers (Frontiers, ELIC / Mission Go) in English. No Arabic taught.
- **Two new letters: د daal and ذ dhaal** (both connect only to the right, like alif and waaw). Board words from the book: دَجاج، جَديد (p.78), ذُباب، خُذ، أَخَذْتُ، يَذوب، ذات (p.80).
- **Listening Exercise 5, p.77:** دَجاج، خُدود (cheeks)، حُدود (borders)، جَديد، أَدَب (literature; Shaami: manners) -> *anta bilaa adab*. Grammar aside: **laa + verb, laysa + noun/pronoun, bilaa = without** (bilaa sabab).
- **Cultural rule:** never hand someone something with *khudh* ('take!') - say *tafaDDal / tafaDDalii*. *akhadhtu A* = I got an A.
- **Unit 4 Vocab (2) slide list:** خُبز، دَجاج، جار، جارة، أَخ، أُخت، جَديد، جَديدة، مَساء الخَير، مَساء النّور، عِندي، لَيسَ عِندي، سُؤال، أُحِبّ، تُحِبّ، تُحِبّين، رَقم تِليفون (+ Shaami lines).
- **Grammar:** 'have' is not a verb - *cindii* = 'at me', takes the possessive endings; -a / ة usually = feminine (jaamica jadiida); first verb *uHibb* (prefix tu- for you; tu- + -iin for a woman); shadda.
- **New required classroom sentence:** *ustaadha, cindii su'aal* - 'from this moment onward, if you have a question, you have to say' it.

## Letters (notes for the letter cards)

- **د** - NEW TODAY (daal). 'The first one, we call it daal.' Connects only to the right, like alif and waaw, so it has only two forms: independent and connecting ('how many forms should we have? Two'). An initial daal is just the independent form ('connecting - we have nothing to connect'). Height: 'alif is the tallest; daal is somewhere between alif and baa'; the tooth [of baa'] is the shortest - make sure you do these differences.' Angle: 'should we write it like a 90 degree angle or like 60 degree angle? That's up to you... just make sure it is not similar to the tooth. I care about the height more.' After a daal the next letter starts over in its initial form ('you cannot make a connection... treat it like the alif and the waaw'): jadiid = jiim + daal + INITIAL yaa' + daal. In Huduud the first daal is connecting (Haa' connects on both sides) and the last is independent (after waaw). Sound: 'the sound of D, the English D, so we're not going to spend much time with the pronunciation.' Words: dajaaj, jadiid, khuduud, Huduud, adab (+ aHdaath, Listening Exercise 5 item 6, not read).
- **ذ** - NEW TODAY (dhaal). 'The transliteration for the dhaal is a dh, small dh. The capital [DH] is for another letter' (ظ). 'Written similar to the daal, the only difference is the dot... daal without, dhaal with - you could remember it that way: dhaal with a dot. They connect the same way, they act the same way, just the sounding of course is different.' Sound = th in 'other' (vs ث, th in 'three'): 'In English, we have TH for both sounds. Do you remember early in the semester you did one drill... is it a th sound or a dh sound? So now you learn the two characters.' Board words (book p.80): dhubaab (flies), khudh (take!), akhadhtu (I took - dhaal with SUKUUN, 'not dhu'), yadhuub (it melts), dhaat (same/self).
- **ث** - Only as the contrast for ذ: th as in 'three' (ث) vs th as in 'other' (ذ). She demonstrated both sets of syllables (tha thu thi / dha dhu dhi) at ~1398-1417 s; the transcripts cannot tell the two sets apart, so the ordering (ث first) is inferred from her sentence order.
- **ا** - 'alif is tall, the tallest' - the height reference for daal. Alif and waaw were the first 'connect only to the right' letters; daal and dhaal join them today. Because alif never connects to the left, the letter after it starts again (final jiim of dajaaj = independent; final baa' of dhubaab = independent). At the start of akhadhtu it carries the hamza with fatHa.
- **ب** - Height reference: 'if I'm writing on the line, baa' for instance is this' - its tooth is the shortest; daal must be taller than the tooth. In dhubaab: initial baa' after dhaal (dhaal doesn't connect left), final baa' independent after alif.
- **و** - Named with alif as the letters that connect only to the right ('alif and waaw connect only to the right'); daal and dhaal 'act the same way'. After waaw the next letter starts over: last daal of Huduud and last baa' of yadhuub are independent forms.
- **ي** - In jadiid the yaa' after daal must be INITIAL: 'you cannot write medial yaa' here' because daal never connects to the left. In yadhuub: 'initial yaa' with the fatHa'.
- **ج** - In dajaaj: jiim initial, alif connecting 'because jiim connects to the left', final jiim independent after alif. Her jiim in words often sounds like 'zh' (Levantine) - whisper heard jadiid as 'Zhadid', Teams heard dajaaj as 'the zaz'.
- **ح** - In Huduud (borders): 'the Haa' connects also both sides', so the daal after it is the connecting form. Contrast khuduud (cheeks) with khaa'.
- **خ** - Starts khudh ('khaa' with a Damma plus dhaal') and is the second letter of akhadhtu ('khaa' here comes with the fatHa'); 'khaa' connects to the left', so the dhaal after it is connecting. Also khubz, khuduud, akh/ukht.
- **ت** - Last letter of akhadhtu (with Damma: -tu) and of dhaat (independent, after alif). Contrast with the word-final ة (taa' marbuuTa) of feminine words: 'it has the taa' at the end' (jaamica/jaamicat).
- **ء** - masaa' ends in a hamza: 'don't forget the glottal stop at the end.' cinda starts with cayn, NOT hamza: 'it's the cayn, not the hamza... don't say ind.' su'aal carries a hamza on a waaw seat; akhadhtu starts with alif-hamza + fatHa.
- **ر** - Not taught yet - 'I'll give you some time until Thursday to process those two letters before I introduce two more'; raa' and zaay come Thursday 10/1 ('I'm supposed to introduce this after the raa' and the zaay').
- **ز** - Not taught yet (Thursday 10/1). It is the last letter of khubz: 'This last letter is the z. Z sound. Z letter.' - write the transliteration for now. She calls the letter 'zayn' (book: zaay).
- **ة** - (taa' marbuuTa, not formally taught yet) The written sign of most feminine words: 'usually the feminine has an -a sound at the end; when you write it, it comes with this marbuuTa here... The majority of the words that end with -a are feminine - the majority, but not all.' Heard as t in jaamicat.
- **ع** - (cayn, not taught yet) Named to fix the first sound of cinda / cindii: 'it's the cayn, not the hamza... exaggerate to remember.'
- **ه** - (haa', not taught yet) First letter of haadhaa / haadhihi: 'you don't know the haa' yet.'
