# Parker's Preferences (living document)

This is where Parker's taste lives. The skill reads it before drafting every card, so anything captured here shapes all future cards. It grows whenever Parker reacts to a card. Every change is a git commit, so we can trace *why* a preference exists and roll back if a change turns out wrong.

How it's used: `card-rules.md` is the hard methodology; this file is Parker's specific tastes layered on top. When they ever conflict, this file wins (it's the more recent, more personal signal).

## Confirmed by Parker
- **PARKER'S OWN ADDITIONS TO A CARD ARE SACRED — never overwrite what you did not write. (2026-07-30, after this system deleted his work.)** He edits his cards constantly and intends to keep doing it: *"frequently I'm gonna add stuff to these cards — notes that I take, mnemonics that I generate — there's stuff that I would add, pictures, all kinds of things to these cards, and if you just deleted that that would be really bad."*
  **What happened:** a session rebuilding the ethical-checklist card noticed its **ETHICS** mnemonic was nowhere in the textbook, concluded a previous run had fabricated it, replaced the card with the book's own wording and ordering, and reported the catch as a win. **The mnemonic was Parker's** — he wrote it himself and reordered the six questions so the letters would spell. It was recovered only because he spoke up. Restored from git.
  **The two failures, both worth remembering:** (1) *"not in the source" was treated as "fabricated"* — but a third author exists, and he is the one who owns the deck. Content absent from the textbook may be **Parker's**, and that possibility must be checked BEFORE any rewrite. (2) The system had no baseline at all: canon files get resynced from live during audits, so they absorb his edits and cannot answer "did we write this?"
  **The rule, now mechanical (`scripts/authorship.py`):** the pipeline fingerprints exactly what it writes, at the moment it writes. Before any later write, the live field is hashed and compared — `owned` may be overwritten; `edited` and `unknown` may NOT. **Silence is not consent:** every card predating the store is `unknown`, and therefore protected. Verified whitespace-only repairs (listify/paragraphize) are still allowed, because they cannot destroy content. When the guard blocks you: read the live value, preserve what he added, or ask him. Never pass the guard just to make a write succeed.
  **And when a card genuinely needs restructuring:** carry his additions forward into the new version rather than dropping them. His mnemonic, his images, his TTS audio, his notes — those survive the rewrite.

- **An attached figure must have a MARGIN around it — never flush to the artwork.** (2026-07-30.) When Parker adds an image by hand he always leaves *"a little bit of space just for the looks and aesthetics… it makes it feel more natural and spacious and simple."* Extraction cuts to the exact artwork bounds, so without a matte a label like *"Parietal bone"* ends touching the image edge and the card reads as cramped. Every study copy is matted at **4% of its normalised long edge** (`build_figure_index.py --pad-pct`), in the plate's own sampled background colour so a dark-ground figure isn't framed in a white halo. **A little, not a lot** — shown 0 / 3 / 5 / 8%, he wanted "not a ton, just enough": 8% read as wasteful, 0% as broken.
- **A figure must be CONGRUENT with the card, not merely near it.** (2026-07-30, caught by Parker while studying ch4.) His test: *"if I see a picture and my first thought is 'why in the world is that picture there?' it leads me to a root of confusion instead of a root of actually succeeding with these flashcards, because I'm sitting here questioning what this picture has to do with the flashcard."* The axis is **congruent vs incongruent, NOT necessary vs unnecessary** — he will happily carry a figure he doesn't need, but not one that sends him hunting for a connection that isn't there. His two examples: a "body language: happy/angry/sad" faces plate on a card about holding your PALMS OUT (*"why am I supposed to have my hands out and then on this other picture there's a crying baby"*), and a radio-tower transmission diagram on a card about CELLULAR TELEPHONES (*"why don't I see a telephone?"*). Both are same-topic, different-subject. Enforced by: zero-coverage matches blocked outright, plus a mandatory judge pass that LOOKS at the plate (SKILL.md Stage 2.95).
- **Pictures: overshoot, don't undershoot.** (2026-07-30.) *"I'd rather overshoot with pictures than undershoot for sure"* — he does not mind a figure that merely illustrates the passage (the developmental age-group photos: he knows what a teenager looks like, but *"there's no problem with having those pictures in the back extra"*). So coverage/archetype grade how STRONG a match is, not whether it happens. The only hard limit is a picture of the WRONG thing, which is why a zero-coverage match must be on the card's own page.
- **Staging (per-source two-deck gate; per-chapter form adopted 2026-07-02, generalized to all sources 2026-07-29):** cards go to that source's `…::claude review` staging deck — NEVER one flat dumping deck (the old single `all::EMT::_Review` was retired for ballooning to ~1,500 cards). Parker PROMOTES keepers into the sibling deck himself after his first review pass. Never write to the promotion deck. Deck names come from `reference/sources.json`; EMT's are case-exact (`Chapter <N>`/`Book Highlights` Title Case, `claude review` lowercase) and must not be restyled.
- **Never drop a *yellow* highlighted span.** Connected/adjacent highlights become ONE card (card-rules Rule 0). The yellow highlight is his decision that it matters. Non-yellow (currently blue) = a note he made for his own interest and explicitly does NOT want to memorize — out of scope, not a miss, don't flag it. (Confirmed 2026-07-29.) **Yellow means card-me whatever the markup style** — he highlights in textbooks but UNDERLINES on lecture slides, and an area-selected figure is an image card. The ONE licensed exception to never-drop: a margin comment countermanding his own mark ("NOT ON THE EXAM", "these are subjective so don't just mem") — honor it, and say so at hand-off.
- **Big lists stay COHESIVE — but they get chunked, not flattened. (Superseded the "no matter the length" clause on 2026-08-02; see below.)** If the items form a genuine cohesive list (parallel members of one set, or the steps of one process), they are ONE concept and must never be scattered into unrelated shards. Never cram *unrelated* facts into one card just to bundle them either. Test before bundling: "are these truly one set, or am I lumping unrelated things together?" (Confirmed 2026-06-29 re: Star of Life = 6 functions, EMS Agenda 2050 = 6 principles.)
  **What changed:** the original wording was "keep them in ONE card *no matter the length* — never split a real list into chunks," and that clause is what produced the 10-element radio-report card. Parker reported it 2026-08-02 as unusable: *"it's telling me to in one go guess all of these things… if I forget one thing, I have to do it all again… what I'll find myself doing is just skipping that flash card."* One concept is still one concept; it is no longer automatically one CARD. The ceiling is card-rules #23 (≤4 uncued answers per group; ≤7 with a mnemonic handle), and past it the set is chunked into named sub-groups on separate notes, each carrying the whole set in a `Roster:` line. He was right about cohesion and the rule was wrong about capacity.

- **A card must be PASSABLE, not merely answerable (2026-08-02, the measurement that settled the list question).** Parker's complaint was not that any individual blank was unfair — it was that the card demanded ten correct answers at once and graded them as one: *"it doesn't know the difference between how well I know that I'm supposed to give a special alert any more than it does that I should radio about the patient's age and sex — all of those things are unique aspects and I want to be quizzed on them."* He is describing all-or-nothing grading, and he is right: at a realistic 90% per item, 0.9^10 is 35%, so a card built from facts he knows cold still fails almost every time, and Anki reschedules the whole thing on its worst item.
  **The evidence, measured on his live collection** (EMT decks only, so same generation runs and same maturity; scored on each card's FIRST review so new-card bias cannot explain it): ordinary 1–2-blank cards failed **56%** at 24.9 s; 3–4-item groups **78%** at 33.3 s; 5–6-item groups **89%** at 42.7 s; 7+ **80%** at 49.3 s. The radio card itself had 5 reviews, **5 "Again," 54 s each — never once answered correctly.**
  **The uncomfortable part, which he should know:** the two cards he named as GOOD are failing too. CHART (5 items, acronym-anchored) is 3/4 Again at 49 s; the five-point handover method is 4/6 Again at 43 s. His instinct about *why* CHART deserves to stay whole is sound — the acronym is the memory anchor and splitting it would destroy that — but "feels right" and "is working" came apart here, and only the review log showed it. Cards at 5–7 items are on probation, not exonerated.

- **He wants the part AND the whole on every card (2026-08-02).** His own proposed fix, adopted verbatim: *"you could extract the table from Zotero and paste the table in the back extra… so I could have the image and once I flip the card, have the understanding of seeing the part and the whole in each flash card."* Every note born from a chunked list carries a `Roster:` line with the complete set in order and its own members bolded.

- **Never "fix" an overloaded list by renumbering it c1..cN — he called this out himself before anyone wrote it down.** *"I don't think that's what we need… if it's a hide-one-but-show-all-the-rest, I can just figure that out."* Sibling numbers on one note generate N cards that each display the other N−1 answers, so the set is recovered by elimination instead of recall. Chunking always means separate NOTES (card-rules #24, HARD-blocked by `sibling_split_leak`).
- **Definitions are two-way by default.** For a term↔meaning fact, write one note as `{{c1::TERM::hint}} is {{c2::tight defining property}}` → Anki makes two cards (name-it from the description, and define-it from the term). Keep the `c2` meaning side CRISP (a few discriminating words, never a long definition). Go single-direction only when just one direction is useful (scenario→name, or word→meaning). Do NOT two-way lists, sequences, numbers, or scenario/application facts. Bury-siblings must be ON for every source's decks so the two halves space across days (`anki_write.py` copies the deck root's preset onto new subdecks automatically). (Decided 2026-06-29, evidence-based: it's AnKing's own §15 "bread-and-butter" pattern, their live decks lean ~73% toward testing the meaning side with crisp answers, and it matches encoding-specificity + minimum-information research. Resolves the directionality gap — the term→meaning direction had been missing.)
- **One shuffled megadeck → every card must answer in ISOLATION.** Parker studies ALL subjects (MCAT, EMT, genetics, chess…) interleaved in ONE deck, so a card can never lean on its neighbors. Give it enough SITUATIONAL/relational context to stand alone — but that context must NEVER be the answer's own definition/synonym (that becomes a crutch he stops testing against). Definitions go in the Back Extra (after the answer); the stem carries situation + a slot-label form hint. The median: a knower can answer from the cue, a non-knower can't decode it. (Articulated 2026-06-29 from the cert→licensure→credentialing pathway card, where "(state authority granted)" leaked "licensure." Full rule: card-rules.md #3.)
- **Tagging (2026-07-02):** tag each card `ch<N>` ONLY. Parker had the `claude_generated` marker removed from every existing card and from the skill (he found it noise). He explicitly likes the chapter tag — keep it; add nothing else.
- **Fresh-pass over patching:** when he wants a chapter redone, wipe the deck and rebuild from scratch rather than editing card-by-card.
- **The Cold-Solve rule — a card he can't answer cold is a "set-up for failure" (confirmed 2026-07-19, three rants).** Parker's single loudest complaint is cards that are *impossible to answer on first sight even when he knows the material*. Every card must pass the cold-solve test: cover the answer, and a knower must be able to produce exactly it from the visible side, having never seen the card. The three shapes he called out, now hard rules (card-rules #16–18, editor checks #18–20, regressions R9–R11):
  - **Open-set (R9):** "guess 3 things her religion opposes" / "you must [some action]" — the answer is one of an unbounded universe. He said: "there are so many things that could fit in that hole… impossible to answer without having first seen the answer." Fix by anchoring the entity/scenario so exactly one answer is forced, not by hoping he guesses the textbook's list.
  - **All-blanks-at-once husk (R10):** two mutually-defining spans hidden under the SAME cloze number, so each blank's only cue is the other (both hidden). He said: "how am I supposed to guess both at the same time… I had no chance, an absolute set-up for failure." Fix: split into c1/c2 so each card shows one span as the anchor.
  - **First-letter-hint copout (R11):** `::r ::k ::s` on a plain list is "literally giving away the answer, an easy copout." First-letter hints are licensed ONLY for a spelled mnemonic the card teaches (SAMPLE). Otherwise drop the hint and expose the shared structure so the crisp discriminator is the recall.
  He wants cards **hard but fair**, never easy-via-giveaway and never impossible-via-starvation. This is the strict form of the older "cue, not crutch" median.

- **The cold-solve test runs PER ROW, and ambiguity is the thing he wants gone (confirmed 2026-07-30, emphatically).** After a third round of ambiguity complaints Parker said: *"I want this ambiguity to be cut out and I want to really be able to feel the cards and be able to do it right… what matters so much more is that this is a systemic issue and that it gets fixed."* Treat every ambiguity report as systemic by default, not as a card to patch. The specific discovery that round: rules 16–18 were enforced at CARD granularity, and multi-row cards fail at ROW granularity — a card can read as answerable while individual rows are self-answering or wide open. **Every row of a list card gets its own cold-solve test.** The three row-level shapes are now card-rules #20–22 (label restates its answer · absolute statement with an unhinted blank · the items visible with only filler clozed), with regressions R15–R17.
  A corollary he stated directly, and which outranks coverage: **a row that answers itself is padding, not coverage — drop it.** He would rather have four rows he must actually produce than seven where three are free.

- **A list of things to guess must be laid out as a list (confirmed 2026-07-30, with before/after screenshots).** When the Text is a lead-in plus a set of items to produce, every row gets its own line with a **blank line between them** (`<br><br>`), never packed with a single `<br>`. His reason: he answers these by first seeing *how many* things he owes, and a packed block hides the count. "It's not in a sentence, it's not doing anything crazy — it's literally just a list." Prose that uses `<br>` between two flowing sentences is NOT a list and stays as-is. Rule: card-rules #19; enforced by `listify()` in `anki_write.py`; regression R14.

## Current defaults (working, but not yet explicitly confirmed — flag if wrong)
- Back Extra opens with a labeled line. The full blessed label set (matches `note-format.md`): **Distinguish** (confusable pairs) · **Pitfall** (a common trap) · **Why** / **Mechanism** (the reason/how) · **Ex** (an example) · **Cue** / **Mnemonic** (a memory hook) · **Pathway** · **Meaning** (expand an acronym). Priority when choosing: Distinguish/Pitfall > Why/Mechanism > Ex > Cue/Mnemonic. (2026-07-01: confirmed the Ch2/Ch3 cards use the wider set well; blessed it here to match the note-format vocabulary.)
- **Margin comments are honored end-to-end.** A `user_comment` question gets answered at hand-off (grounded in source); "look more into this" gets `needs_human_check` + a specific to-do; "know all of these" makes the list exhaustive. (2026-07-01.)
- Confusable terms (certification/licensure/credentialing, primary/secondary prevention, online/off-line) get cross-linking *Distinguish* lines.
- "such as" / open-ended lists are NOT carded as closed memorize-these lists.
- **Breaks between chunks:** when a Text or Back Extra holds two or more distinct pieces of info, separate them so the card is scannable, not a run-on block. (Parker: "I kinda want there to be breaks inbtw info.") Don't break inside a single flowing sentence.
- **Back Extra components get a full paragraph break `<br><br>` (2026-07-02).** Between two distinct labeled components — e.g. a `Distinguish:` line and a `Pitfall:` line — put `<br><br>`, NOT a single `<br>`. Parker wants real white space between the parts so his eye can bounce from one to the next; a single line break packs them too tight. He confirmed this with before/after screenshots. It's purely aesthetic but he cares about it. `anki_write.py` enforces this mechanically (it normalizes any run of `<br>` in Back Extra to exactly `<br><br>` at write time), so it holds even if a card is drafted with single breaks.
- **Italics enabled** for selective emphasis/contrast (Parker asked for this repeatedly across his ChatGPT prompt versions; his gold cards use it). Same restraint as bold — the load-bearing word only.
